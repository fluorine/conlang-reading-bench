Can AI read a new language?

Give a model a tiny story in a made-up language, then ask who has the key. How much help does it need to follow the story?

A story from the test

Cy numa vema

Ada numa kato

Ada-ak kato Bo-es poma

Bo-ak kato Cy-es poma

Who holds the key at the end?

Reveal the answer

Cy. Ada starts with the key, gives it to Bo, and Bo gives it to Cy. The coin stays with Cy the whole time.

Pilot results

What changed with clues?

GPT-4.1 mini answered about half the questions with minimal clues and over three quarters with the full grammar. GPT-4o mini reached half with the full grammar.

65.63%GPT-4.1 mini
45.83%GPT-4o mini

Overall scores average the three invented-language conditions equally. English is scored separately.

The method

One story, four ways to read it.

Follow the objects

Three people exchange two objects in two transfers. Four questions check who received an object, who held it earlier, and who holds each object at the end. A simulator records every change and supplies the answer key.

Change the clues

Each story gets invented words, word order, and word endings. Minimal clues give one word meaning and an example. A glossary adds every word meaning. Full grammar explains word order and who gives and receives. An English version checks ordinary story tracking.

Compare fairly

Both models see identical stories and questions, with a fresh conversation for each condition. The pilot used GPT-4.1 mini (April 2025) and GPT-4o mini (July 2024) at default effort, temperature zero, and a 64-token answer limit.

How to read the gap

GPT-4.1 mini led by 19.79 percentage points overall. The 95% interval across these stories was 11.46 to 28.13 points. Both models fell short of the 90% English target during development, so the comparison is exploratory.
